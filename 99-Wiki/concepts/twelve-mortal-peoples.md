# twelve mortal peoples

## Definition

The phrase **twelve mortal peoples** denotes the collective of human societies that were created by the Patron Gods through the enactment of survival covenants during the mythic event known as Nightfall. It functions as a term that groups together all mortal cultures whose origins are attributed to this divine covenantal act.

## Key Characteristics

- **Divine Origin**: Each of the twelve peoples traces its lineage to the Patron Gods’ intervention at Nightfall.  
- **Covenantal Basis**: Their existence is bound by the *survival covenants* that prescribe mutual obligations between the deities and humanity.  
- **Cultural Unity**: Although distinct in customs and geography, the peoples share a common mythic heritage and theological framework.  
- **Numerical Symbolism**: The number twelve reflects cosmological significance within the lore, often associated with completeness and order.

## Applications

- **Mythological Narrative**: Used to explain the genesis of humanity and the moral obligations of mortal societies to the divine.  
- **Ritual Practice**: Serves as a foundational concept in rites that invoke the Patron Gods for protection and guidance.  
- **World‑building**: Provides a structural basis for the political and cultural landscape in storytelling, gaming, or scholarly analysis of the lore.  
- **Comparative Religion**: Offers a reference point for analyzing similar covenantal creation myths across different traditions.
