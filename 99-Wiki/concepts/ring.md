# Ring

## Definition

The Ring is a broad geographic belt that encapsulates the area known as the [[entities/the-rise|Rise]]. It functions as a foundational architectural and geographical standard that defines the spatial organization and progression of the world through a concentric, layered model moving from the core toward the external periphery.

## Key Characteristics

- **Geographic Encapsulation**: Encloses the central region known as the [[entities/the-rise|Rise]].
- **Spatial Buffer**: Operates as a border region or protective belt between the central territories and the outer reaches.
- **Topographical Significance**: Serves as a fundamental reference point for navigation and regional classification.
- **Concentric Structure**: Organized in distinct radial tiers emanating from a central point.
- **Fixed Sequence**: The progression follows a specific order: Center (Vigil/Rise), First (Hearth), Second (Gloam), Third (Murk), Fourth (Night), Fifth (Rim), and Beyond (Dark).
- **Progression Logic**: Functions as the primary roadmap for world-building, narrative pacing, and game level design.
- **Systemic Purpose**: Describes the outward expansion of Darkness, where gameplay is managed through radial progression tiers supplemented by local world modifiers.

## Applications

- Used as a primary frame of reference for defining the limits of the [[entities/the-rise|Rise]].
- Functions as a conceptual and physical boundary in cartography and regional studies.
- Serves as a buffer zone within the geopolitical landscape.
- **Cartography**: Used to structure the layout of the world map.
- **Game Design**: Defines the difficulty curve and zones for player advancement, though the ring number should not be treated as a perfectly fixed difficulty.
- **Narrative Pacing**: Dictates the movement of plot events as they transition through the geographical rings.
